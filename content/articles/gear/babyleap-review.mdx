## Drawbacks first

**The trial is short.** Seven days is not long enough to evaluate an app whose value
is a _weekly_ plan. You get one cycle.

**The best feature is paywalled.** The AI assistant handles your 2am "is this normal"
questions better than a search engine does, and it is premium-only. We can defend
that commercially, and it still irritates.

**Monthly pricing is bad value.** The annual plan is reasonable for what it is. We
priced the monthly plan to push you toward annual, and it works.

**We are asking for your baby's data.** We wrote a readable privacy policy and we do
not sell the data. Read it yourself rather than taking our word for it, given who is
writing this.

## Worth paying for

**Milestones as ranges.** We rate this design decision above everything else in the
app, and most competitors get it wrong. BabyLeap shows development as "most babies do
this between X and Y" rather than as a date your baby can miss. Parents get anxious
using these apps, so the framing matters more than any feature.

**The weekly plan is specific.** Activities are concrete and tied to what your baby is
working on, rather than generic "talk to your baby" encouragement. If anything
justifies the subscription, this does.

You also log feeds, sleep and nappies alongside development, so there is nothing to
reconcile between two apps at 3am. Clinicians review the content before it ships, to
the same standard we hold our own articles to.

## Who it is not for

If your problem is **sleep**, a dedicated sleep app will serve you better; BabyLeap's
sleep logging is competent rather than exceptional.

If you dislike subscriptions on principle, a one-off purchase app will annoy you less,
even if it does less.

If you want reassurance about a specific developmental concern, no app will give it to
you. Take it to your paediatrician, and read our guide to <InlineArticleLink slug="when-do-babies-roll-over">developmental ranges and the signs worth raising</InlineArticleLink> first.
