Most third-trimester checklists are shopping lists with a countdown attached,
giving wipe warmers and nappy stackers the same billing as the car seat. We
ordered this one by how much each item changes your first six weeks.

<Callout type="warning">

  **Call your provider immediately, at any hour, for:** reduced or changed fetal movement, any
  bleeding, a gush or persistent trickle of fluid, a severe or persistent headache, visual changes
  or spots, sudden swelling of the face and hands, upper abdominal pain, or contractions before 37
  weeks. Never sleep on reduced movement.

</Callout>

## Four things that matter most

**1. A correctly installed car seat.** You cannot leave hospital without one, and
parents install a large share of seats wrong. Fit yours well before your due date
and have someone check it; many fire services and hospitals do this free. Practise
the harness in daylight, before you need it in a dark car park.

**2. A safe sleep space.** A cot, crib or bassinet with a firm flat mattress and a
fitted sheet, and nothing else in it. That is the whole specification. See our
summary of the current <InlineArticleLink slug="aap-safe-sleep-update">safe sleep guidance</InlineArticleLink>.

**3. A feeding plan and the equipment for it.** If breastfeeding, find your local
lactation support _before_ you need them at 2am on day four. If formula feeding,
have bottles, a way to sterilise, and a tin in the house. If undecided, prepare
for both. That costs little and removes a decision from your worst week.

**4. A postnatal support plan.** Parents skip this one more than any other item,
and it repays more than the rest. Settle now who brings food, who holds the baby
while you shower, and who you phone at the point of struggling, then write their
names down. A vague offer of help will not survive week two.

## Worth doing, in the next few weeks

- **Pack the bag by 36 weeks.** Longer than you think for you, less than you think
  for the baby. Your own pillow, a phone charger with a long cable, snacks, and
  something to wear home that fits at around six months pregnant.
- Write your birth preferences on one page rather than five, and include what you
  want if things change course.
- Sort the paperwork: parental leave, paediatrician chosen, pharmacy located,
  freezer stocked.
- **Take an infant CPR class.** You will probably never use it. Spend the two
  hours.

## Safe to postpone

Almost everything. Leave the nursery unfinished; your baby will sleep in your room
for months. You need far fewer newborn-size clothes than anyone has sold you, and
babies outgrow them fast.

Buy the nappy bin, the bottle warmer, the wipe warmer, the sleep gadgets, most
monitors and anything promising a longer-sleeping newborn once you know which
problems you have, or never.

## Feeding and development, later

Leave solids alone; that is a six-month problem, and our guide to <InlineArticleLink slug="starting-solids-first-foods">starting solids</InlineArticleLink> will
keep. Milestone tracking can wait too. Spend the third trimester on the car seat,
the sleep space, the feeding plan and the people.
