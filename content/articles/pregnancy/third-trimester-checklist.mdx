Third-trimester checklists tend to be shopping lists with a countdown attached.
Wipe warmers and nappy stackers get equal billing with the car seat.

This one is ordered by how much each item actually affects the first six weeks.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Call your provider immediately, at any hour, for:** reduced or changed fetal movement, any
  bleeding, a gush or persistent trickle of fluid, a severe or persistent headache, visual changes
  or spots, sudden swelling of the face and hands, upper abdominal pain, or contractions before 37
  weeks. Reduced movement is never something to sleep on.
</Callout>

## The four things that genuinely matter

**1. A correctly installed car seat.** You cannot leave hospital without one,
and a large share of seats are installed incorrectly. Fit it well before your
due date and have it checked — many fire services and hospitals offer this free.
Practise the harness in daylight, not in a car park at midnight.

**2. A safe sleep space.** A cot, crib or bassinet with a firm flat mattress and
a fitted sheet. Nothing else in it. That is the entire specification, and it is
the one where getting it right matters most. See our summary of the current

<InlineArticleLink slug="aap-safe-sleep-update">safe sleep guidance</InlineArticleLink>.

**3. A feeding plan and the equipment for it.** If breastfeeding, know who your
local lactation support is _before_ you need them at 2am on day four. If
formula feeding, have bottles, a way to sterilise, and a tin in the house. If
undecided, prepare for both — that costs very little and removes a decision from
your worst week.

**4. A postnatal support plan.** The single highest-return item on this list and
the one most often skipped. Who is bringing food? Who can hold the baby while
you shower? Who do you call if you are struggling? Write actual names down.
Vague offers of help do not survive contact with week two.

## Worth doing, in the next few weeks

- **Pack the bag by 36 weeks.** Longer than you think for you, less than you
  think for the baby. Your own pillow, phone charger with a long cable, snacks,
  something to wear home that fits at around six months pregnant.
- **Write down your preferences, and stay flexible.** A one-page summary is more
  useful than a five-page plan. Include what you want if things change course.
- **Sort the practicalities.** Parental leave paperwork, paediatrician chosen,
  pharmacy located, freezer stocked.
- **Take an infant CPR class.** You will very likely never use it. It is worth
  two hours anyway.

## What can wait

Almost everything. The nursery does not need to be finished — your baby will
sleep in your room for months. You need far fewer newborn-size clothes than you
have been sold, and babies grow out of them alarmingly fast.

Nappy bins, bottle warmers, wipe warmers, sleep gadgets, most monitors, and the
entire category of products promising to make a newborn sleep longer can be
bought later, if at all, once you know what your actual problems are.

## Feeding and development, later

You do not need to plan solids yet — that is a six-month problem, and our guide
to <InlineArticleLink slug="starting-solids-first-foods">starting solids</InlineArticleLink>
will keep. The same goes for milestone tracking. The third trimester is for the
car seat, the sleep space, the feeding plan and the people.
