Open any parenting app and you will meet the wake window chart: a grid that puts
a six-week-old at 60 minutes awake and leaves you counting the minutes you
overshot when bedtime goes badly. Sleep consultants built those charts, and
almost none of them cite anything.

## Consultants invented the numbers

Wake windows are a rule of thumb rather than a clinical measure. Sleep
consultants and parent forums produced them, anchored to something real: sleep
pressure builds through the day, and an overtired baby does settle less easily.
The idea holds up. The specific numbers carry more authority than anyone has
earned for them.

## What the research supports

The
[American Academy of Pediatrics](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/default.aspx)
is the reference point for the parts that are reasonably well established:

- Newborns need a lot of sleep, and the spread between healthy babies is wide.
  The commonly cited figure is 14 to 17 hours across 24 hours, with normal
  variation on either side.
- Newborn sleep arrives in short bouts around the clock. Babies consolidate
  night sleep as they develop; training does not produce it.
- Babies show observable cues before they tip into overtiredness, and those cues
  differ from baby to baby.

No study establishes that a particular number of minutes is correct for a
particular week of life.

## Read your baby

Watch for a fixed stare, disengagement, jerky movements, that first yawn. Those signals
come from your baby rather than from an average of other people's babies, and
they arrive before any timer does. Treat the chart as a rough prompt to start
watching, then let your baby decide what happens next.

## When to raise it with a clinician

Babies vary. Call your own paediatrician about any of these:

- A newborn you repeatedly struggle to wake for feeds
- Poor weight gain, or fewer wet nappies than you expect
- Breathing that looks laboured during sleep
- A sudden change in sleep that holds, with no obvious cause

Those are clinical questions, and no amount of rescheduling naps will answer
them.
