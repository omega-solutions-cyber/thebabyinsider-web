At some point around four months, a baby who had been sleeping in reassuringly
long stretches starts waking every forty minutes, all night, for no apparent
reason. The internet calls this a regression.

It is the opposite of a regression. It is a permanent step forward, and
understanding that changes what you do about it.

<KeyTakeaways>
  - Infant sleep **matures** at around four months into adult-like cycles with distinct stages and
  brief arousals between them. - The waking is not a problem to be fixed; the difficulty is that
  your baby now surfaces between cycles and has to get back down. - Sleep will not return to the
  newborn pattern, because that pattern was developmentally temporary. - Disruption usually settles
  within two to six weeks.
</KeyTakeaways>

## What actually changes

Newborn sleep has essentially two states and moves between them without much
ceremony. Around three to five months, that reorganises into cycles with
distinct stages, closer to the structure adults have.

Adult sleep cycles end with a brief arousal — you surface, register that
everything is fine, and drop back down without remembering it. Your baby now
does the same thing, roughly every forty to sixty minutes, and does not yet know
how to complete the second half.

That is the whole phenomenon. Nothing has gone wrong.

<Callout type="doctor">
  Because this is a maturational change, "waiting it out" does not fix it in the way waiting out a
  growth spurt does. What resolves is your baby's ability to link cycles — and that is something you
  can support.
</Callout>

## What actually helps

There is no approach that works for every family, and anyone claiming otherwise
is selling something. These are the levers with the best support:

**Practise falling asleep at the start of the night.** The transition your baby
needs to learn is the one from awake to asleep. Whatever method fits your
family, doing it at bedtime rather than at 3am is easier for everyone.

**Watch daytime sleep.** Overtiredness makes cycle transitions harder, not
easier. Days that end in a badly overtired baby usually produce worse nights.

**Keep the environment constant.** If your baby falls asleep with you rocking
them and surfaces alone in a dark room, the mismatch is what triggers the full
wake-up. Whatever conditions are present at the start should be present at 2am.

**Give it time.** Two to six weeks is normal. Changing approach every four days
because it "isn't working" is the most common way to extend it.

## When it is not the regression

Assume it is developmental, but not indefinitely. Talk to your paediatrician if:

- Waking comes with poor feeding, poor weight gain, or fewer wet nappies
- Your baby seems in pain rather than unsettled
- There is snoring, pauses in breathing, or laboured breathing during sleep
- The disruption has run well past six weeks with no direction of travel
- **You are not coping.** Parental exhaustion at this stage is a legitimate
