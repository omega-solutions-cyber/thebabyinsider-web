Your baby slept in long, reassuring stretches. Now, somewhere around four months,
they wake every forty minutes all night for no reason you can find. The internet
calls this a regression.

Your baby has moved forward, permanently, and that changes what you should do
about it.

<KeyTakeaways>

- At around four months your baby's brain **rebuilds** sleep into adult-like cycles, with distinct stages and a brief arousal between each one.
- Your baby now surfaces between cycles and cannot yet get back down without help.
- Newborn sleep will not come back. That pattern was always temporary.
- Disruption usually settles within two to six weeks.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Adult-like cycles arrive

A newborn has two sleep states and slides between them without much ceremony.
Between three and five months your baby's brain builds those into cycles with
distinct stages, closer to the structure you have as an adult.

Your own cycles end with a brief arousal. You surface, register that everything is
fine, and drop back down without remembering any of it. Your baby now does the
same thing every forty to sixty minutes and cannot yet manage the second half of
it. Nothing has gone wrong.

<Callout type="doctor">

  You can wait out a growth spurt. This is a maturational change, so waiting does less. Your baby
  learns to link cycles, and you can help them do it.

</Callout>

## Where to put your effort

No approach works for every family, and anyone who claims otherwise is selling
something. The best-supported move is to practise the awake-to-asleep transition
at the start of the night rather than at 3am. Your baby has to learn it
somewhere, and whichever method suits your family, bedtime is the cheapest place
to run it.

Daytime sleep matters here too, because overtiredness makes cycle transitions
harder. A day that ends with a badly overtired baby tends to produce a worse
night.

**Keep the conditions constant.** Your baby falls asleep while you rock them,
then surfaces alone in a dark room and wakes fully, because the room is not what
it was. Whatever you set up at the start should still hold at 2am.

**Give it time.** Two to six weeks is normal. Parents who switch approach every
four days because it "isn't working" are the ones who stretch it out longest.

## Signs it is something else

Assume development, but not forever. Talk to your paediatrician if:

- Waking comes with poor feeding, poor weight gain, or fewer wet nappies
- Your baby seems to be in pain rather than unsettled
- You hear snoring, pauses in breathing, or laboured breathing during sleep
- Six weeks have passed and nothing is moving in either direction
- **You are not coping.** Clinicians treat parental exhaustion at this stage as a
  real concern, so raise it. See our piece on <InlineArticleLink slug="parental-burnout-first-year">burnout in the first year</InlineArticleLink>.
