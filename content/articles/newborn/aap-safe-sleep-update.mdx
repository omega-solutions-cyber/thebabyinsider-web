Updated infant safe sleep guidance has been moving through parenting groups for
the past fortnight, usually stripped of context and often with a product
recommendation attached. Several of the claims circulating are wrong.

Here is the short, accurate version.

<KeyTakeaways>

- The core recommendations have **not** changed: back to sleep, firm flat surface, own sleep space, nothing else in it.
- The clearest guidance is on **weighted sleep products** — weighted swaddles, sacks and blankets are advised against for infants.
- Inclined sleepers and any surface that is not firm and flat remain unsuitable for sleep.
- Room sharing without bed sharing is still recommended, ideally for at least the first six months.

</KeyTakeaways>

## What has not changed

The foundation is the same as it has been for years, and the repetition is the
point — these are the recommendations with the strongest evidence behind them.

- **Back to sleep**, for every sleep, by every caregiver, until the first birthday
- **Firm, flat, non-inclined surface** designed for infant sleep
- **Nothing else in the sleep space** — no pillows, bumpers, loose blankets or
  soft toys
- **Room sharing without bed sharing**, ideally for at least six months
- Avoid smoke exposure, and avoid overheating

<Callout type="doctor">

  Most of what parents are told is "new" in safe sleep is a restatement of existing recommendations.
  If a post pairs a guidance update with a product to buy, treat it as marketing until proven
  otherwise.

</Callout>

## Where the emphasis has sharpened

**Weighted sleep products.** This is the clearest message. Weighted swaddles,
sleep sacks and blankets are advised against for infants, on the basis that
added weight on an infant's chest is a plausible risk and the safety case for
these products has not been made. Several large retailers have withdrawn them.

**Inclined and non-flat surfaces.** Any product that positions a sleeping infant
at an incline is not a safe sleep surface. This includes swings, bouncers,
loungers and car seats outside the car. Babies fall asleep in all of these
constantly — the guidance is to move them to a flat firm surface when you
reasonably can, not to panic.

**Sofas and armchairs.** Falling asleep with an infant on a sofa or armchair
carries substantially higher risk than a bed. If night feeds leave you fighting
sleep, feeding in a bed cleared of pillows and bedding is the safer fallback.

## What this means if you already own something

If you have a weighted sack, stop using it for sleep. If you have an inclined
sleeper, stop using it for sleep. Neither of those is a reason for retrospective
guilt — guidance changes precisely because the evidence base moves.

## The honest caveat

This is general information about published recommendations, not advice about
your baby. Infants with reflux, prematurity, or particular medical conditions
sometimes have individualised guidance from their own clinician — and that
guidance wins over anything you read here.
