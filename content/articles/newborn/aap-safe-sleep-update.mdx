Parents have been passing updated safe sleep guidance around their groups for a
fortnight, stripped of context and often with a product recommendation attached.
Some of them now repeat claims the guidance never made.

<KeyTakeaways>

- The core recommendations have **not** changed: back to sleep, firm flat surface, own sleep space, nothing else in it.
- The clearest guidance covers **weighted sleep products**. Weighted swaddles, sacks and blankets are advised against for infants.
- Inclined sleepers, and any surface that is not firm and flat, remain unsuitable for sleep.
- Room sharing without bed sharing still stands, ideally for at least the first six months.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Nothing here is new

These recommendations carry the strongest evidence of anything here, and they have
held for years. Read the list again even though you know it.

- **Back to sleep**, for every sleep, by every caregiver, until the first birthday
- **Firm, flat, non-inclined surface** designed for infant sleep
- **Nothing else in the sleep space.** No pillows, bumpers, loose blankets or soft
  toys
- **Room sharing without bed sharing**, ideally for at least six months
- Avoid smoke exposure, and avoid overheating

<Callout type="doctor">

  Almost every "new" safe sleep rule you hear restates an existing recommendation. Treat any post
  that pairs a guidance update with something to buy as marketing until the poster proves otherwise.

</Callout>

## Reviewers sharpened three points

Weighted sleep products carry the clearest message. The AAP advises against
weighted swaddles, sleep sacks and blankets for infants, on the basis that added
weight on an infant's chest is a plausible risk and no manufacturer has made the
safety case. Several large retailers have withdrawn them.

**Inclined and non-flat surfaces.** Any product that positions a sleeping infant
at an incline is not a safe sleep surface. That covers swings, bouncers, loungers
and car seats outside the car. Babies fall asleep in all of them. Move your baby
to a flat firm surface when you reasonably can, and skip the panic.

**Sofas and armchairs.** Falling asleep with an infant on a sofa or armchair
carries substantially higher risk than a bed does. If night feeds leave you
fighting sleep, feed in a bed you have cleared of pillows and bedding.

## If you already own one of these

If you have a weighted sack, stop using it for sleep. Same for an inclined
sleeper. Skip the retrospective guilt: researchers published new evidence and the
committees updated their advice, which is the process working.

Once your baby starts rolling, stop swaddling, whatever the swaddle is made of.

the timing and what to use instead.

## One caveat

This is general information about published recommendations rather than advice
about your baby. Infants with reflux, prematurity, or particular medical
conditions sometimes have individualised guidance from their own clinician.
Follow that over anything you read here.
